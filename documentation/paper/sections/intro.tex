\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

This work is largely motivated by personal interest in the topic which has been spawned by various online resources. For example, there is a series of videos that documents the development of the ``JDH-8'' architecture and subsequently computer\cite{jdh8_1,jdh8_2,jdh8_3,jdh8_4}. The source code and design files for the project are also available publicly\cite{jdh8_repo}. Another example is the excellent video series ``Building a 6502 computer from scratch'' by Ben Eater\cite{eater6502}. These and similar video documentations sparked an interest to develop and implement a custom computer architecture from first principles in order to gain a deep and intuitive understanding for the mechanisms at play.

This interest was built upon in the computer science program at the Cooperate State University (DHBW), where the lectures ``Digitaltechnik'' and ``Rechnerarchitektur'' teach fundamentals of modern digital computing systems. Incidentally, both lectures can benefit from a quality visualisation of the course material. ``Digitaltechnik'' imparts fundamentals of digital circuits, while ``Rechnerarchitektur'' shows the internals of digital processors on a higher level of abstraction. A model of a simple \ac{cpu} could serve as a helpful bridge between the two levels of abstraction. From the perspective of ``Digitaltechnik'', individual modules of the \ac{cpu} are especially interesting. For example, registers or individual arithmetic operations can be shown on their physical counterparts. From the perspective of ``Rechnerarchitektur'', the composition and interconnection of the modules is more relevant. Here, the datapath or the control logic can be visualized. Both lectures may benefit from a visualisation of the order of steps within a digital system. Since the implementation is on a gate level, minimizing the number of gates is the most important metric when designing an implementation of the \ac{isa}.

Implementing such a device and using it as an educational tool is the core motivation for this work. Additionally, the assignment offers an enormous potential for learning new concepts. It touches upon a broad spectrum of technical topics, whose fundamentals have already been taught. With this project, these foundations can be sharpened and expanded upon. Planning and executing a complex technical project independently also offers opportunities for personal development.

\subsection{Goals}

First and foremost, this work aims to create a tangible, illustrative implementation of a simple digital computing system. This is intended for visualizing the processes withing a \ac{cpu}. Additionally, the \ac{isa} shall be fully documented to serve as a jumping-off-point for future modification or extension. The physical build must have a user interface to allow real-time interaction with the system. It must also be suitable to demonstrate the individual processes that occur during each clock cycle. The hardware shall be tested for performance and correctness in order to compare it to other, similarly complex systems.

\subsection{Approach}

Initially, this work explains core concepts and technical expressions needed for understanding later sections. This includes a brief overview of how a digital processor operates and how different ones can be differentiated. An introduction to boolean algebra is followed by an explanation of what an \ac{isa} is.

Afterwards, the development of the custom computer is documented. This is presented in the order it was initially planned and executed in. The initial step is to define the desired behavior of the device in the form of an \ac{isa}. This includes an explanation of the guiding design principles and an overview of the instruction set provided to programmers. Next, this instruction set is translated into a high level datapath that connects a set of abstract modules.

The second step of implementation aims to validate the architecture and datapath design by implementing a software emulator and simulator at the level of individual logic gates. An assembler is developed in order to translate human readable program source code into executable binaries. Using these three components, programs are developed to explore the capabilities of the system. At this stage, automated testing is applied to verify the expected behavior.

The final stage of implementation is hardware development. In this phase, the hardware equivalent of the software simulator and emulator is designed. This section documents the changes that must be made in order to translate a rather abstract, purely logical boolean circuit into an electrical schematic and subsequently \ac{pcb}. It explains the design methodology and approach to \ac{pcb} design and layout, as well as purely electromechanical necessities such as power management, hardware mounting, connectors and similar elements.

A retrospective summary forms the closing section of this work. It evaluates the progress of the implementation and names difficulties and problems that occured during the course of the project. This is followed by an outlook, showing areas of potential that future works may use as a jumping-off-point.
